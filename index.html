How it decides

Read the rules

For each second the page takes the average C/N0 of the 4 strongest satellites and compares it with the normal level for your log (the 75th percentile, or the slider value).

Jamming: severe C/N0 drop and the AGC fell too (the receiver turned its gain down because extra radio power arrived), or no satellites at all while AGC fell.

Interference: degraded C/N0 with an AGC drop, or, on phones without AGC, even the high-elevation satellites are badly weakened.

Blockage: C/N0 dropped but the AGC stayed put. Trees, roofs, buildings, tunnels.

No signal: no satellites and no AGC evidence either way.

No noise gauge: phones on Android 6 ("basic mode", old satellite API), Android 7, and some newer phones don't report AGC. For those seconds the verdict rests on the signal drop alone, so they can show Interference but never Jamming. Their points have a dashed outline.

Unusual: 6+ satellites all very strong and nearly identical (a spoofing hint, not proof).

Seconds without a GPS position get the phone's Wi-Fi/cell position, or are placed along the route between the known positions before and after (hollow markers).

Airplane squares: a plane counts as "poor GPS" when it reports position accuracy worse than about 93 m (NACp under 8) or a low integrity number (NIC under 7), or when it stopped sending GPS positions. Squares are 0.5° (about 50 km). Green: under 2% of planes poor. Yellow: 2–10%. Red: over 10%, which usually means jamming. Grey: fewer than 3 planes, too few to tell. A square needs at least 2 poor planes to turn yellow or red, because a single plane (often a small helicopter) can report poor GPS all the time without any jamming. Planes on the ground and older transponders are ignored because they often report poor accuracy for harmless reasons.
